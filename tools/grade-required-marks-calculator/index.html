              What Is a Weighted Grade?

              A weighted grade is a course grade built from several assessments — homework, quizzes, midterms, a final exam — where each one counts for a different share of the total. Instead of every assessment mattering equally, a heavier assessment like a final exam pulls the overall grade more than a lighter one like a single quiz.

              This calculator handles five common situations around weighted grades in one connected tool: working out your current grade from completed work, finding the score you need on a remaining final, testing what-if scenarios, finding your best possible outcome, and comparing several target grades at once.

              How Is Your Current Grade Calculated?

              Each completed assessment contributes to your grade by its score multiplied by its weight:

              Weighted Contribution = Score × Weight (weight expressed as a decimal)

              Adding up every contribution gives your Current Contribution — the percentage points you have already banked toward the full course. Dividing that total by your Completed Weight (the sum of the weights entered so far) gives your Current Grade: your average across the work finished so far. For example, 90% on a 20%-weighted assignment and 80% on a 30%-weighted midterm contribute 18 and 24 points — a Current Contribution of 42 points over a Completed Weight of 50%, for a Current Grade of 84%.

              How to Calculate the Required Final Exam Score

              Required Final mode works backward from your target course grade to tell you exactly what you need on the exam that is still ahead of you:

              Required Final Score = (Target − Current Grade × (1 − Final Weight)) ÷ Final Weight

              If the result is zero or below, your target is already secured regardless of the final. If it lands between 0% and 100%, that is the score you need. If it comes out to exactly 100%, only a perfect score will do — and if it exceeds 100%, the target cannot be reached through the final alone, no matter how well you score.

              How What-If Grade Calculations Work

              What-If Final mode runs the required-final formula in reverse: instead of solving for a score from a target, you supply a hypothetical final exam score and see exactly where it would leave your overall grade:

              Projected Final Grade = Current Grade × (1 − Final Weight) + Expected Final Score × Final Weight

              This is useful for testing scenarios — "what if I get a 70 instead of an 85?" — without committing to a single target grade. The projection only reflects the current grade, final weight and expected score you enter; it never assumes a target you haven't provided.

              How to Calculate Your Best Possible Grade

              Best Possible mode finds the absolute ceiling for your final course grade — the outcome if every remaining assessment goes perfectly:

              Best Possible Grade = Current Grade × Completed Weight + 100 × Remaining Weight

              Completed Weight here is simply 1 minus your Remaining Weight. If your remaining weight is 0% — meaning the course is already fully graded — there is nothing left to score, so your best possible grade is the same as your current grade.

              How to Set a Realistic Grade Target

              Target Comparison mode checks five common target grades — 70%, 75%, 80%, 85% and 90% — against your current position in one pass, using the same mathematics as Required Final for each target. Instead of testing one number at a time, you immediately see which targets are already achieved, which are realistically achievable, which need a perfect remaining performance, and which are mathematically out of reach.

              This makes it easy to pick a target that is ambitious but grounded in what your remaining assessment weight can actually deliver, rather than guessing.

              Common Weighted Grade Mistakes

              Mistake 1 — Confusing Current Grade with Current Contribution. Current Grade is your average over completed work only; Current Contribution is how many points that work has already added toward the full 100% course. They are only the same number once every assessment is complete.

              Mistake 2 — Entering weights that add up to more than 100%. Every assessment weight should reflect its actual share of the total course grade — the full set should never exceed 100%.

              Mistake 3 — Assuming any target is reachable. If the required score on your remaining work exceeds 100%, no result can close the gap. Check Required Final or Target Comparison before assuming a goal is realistic.

              Mistake 4 — Forgetting that Best Possible is a ceiling, not a prediction. It assumes a perfect score on everything remaining — treat it as the upper bound of what's mathematically possible, not an expected outcome.

              Tip: Recheck your weights against the syllabus. A common source of error is entering the wrong weight for an assessment — confirm the breakdown in your course syllabus before calculating.

              Tips for Planning Your Final Grade

              Revisit Current Grade mode after every new result — even one new assessment shifts your completed weight and changes what's still needed. Once you know where you stand, use Required Final to check a specific target, or Target Comparison to see several targets side by side without repeating the calculation manually.

              If a target turns out to be out of reach, What-If Final lets you test more realistic final exam scores to see where you would actually land, and Best Possible tells you the absolute ceiling so you know the honest upper limit before setting your next goal.
