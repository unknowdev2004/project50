What Should You Focus On?

  • Hit your protein target first. Protein prevents muscle loss, keeps you full and is the hardest macro to overeat. Aim for your recommended amount on most days.

  • Reach your calorie target second. Total calories determine whether you lose, maintain or gain weight. Once protein is set, use fat and carbs to fill the remaining calorie budget.

  • Use carbs and fats flexibly. Both macros serve important roles but can be adjusted based on preference, hunger and training schedule. Keep fat above its minimum; let carbs flex within their range.

Why These Numbers?

Protein

Supports muscle recovery, prevents muscle loss during a calorie deficit, and keeps you feeling full. Higher intakes are especially important when losing fat or building muscle.

Fat

Essential for hormone production, fat-soluble vitamin absorption (A, D, E, K), and brain function. Going too low on fat disrupts hormonal health. Never drop below your minimum.

Carbohydrates

Your body's primary energy source. Carbs fuel workouts, support recovery, and regulate mood. Because protein and fat targets are fixed, carbs fill the remaining calorie budget and are the most flexible macro to adjust.

About the Macro Calculator

Macronutrients — commonly called "macros" — are the three categories of nutrients that provide calories: protein, fat and carbohydrates. Every food you eat is made up of some combination of these three. Understanding how much of each you need is the foundation of precise nutrition, whether you are trying to lose body fat, maintain your current weight, or build muscle.

Protein is the building block of muscle tissue. It supports repair after exercise, prevents muscle loss during a calorie deficit, and helps keep you full throughout the day. Protein provides 4 kcal per gram.

Fat is essential for hormone production, brain function, and the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E and K). Fat provides 9 kcal per gram — more than twice the density of protein or carbohydrates — so a small amount goes a long way toward your daily calorie target.

Carbohydrates are your body's preferred energy source, particularly during high-intensity exercise. They fuel workouts, support recovery and help regulate mood. Carbohydrates provide 4 kcal per gram. Because protein and fat targets are relatively fixed for a given body weight and goal, carbohydrates fill the remaining calorie budget and are the most flexible macro to adjust day to day.

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation to determine your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) and Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE), then applies evidence-based macro ratios tailored to your goal. If you know your body fat percentage, the tool calculates macros relative to your lean body mass for greater precision.

How To Use This Macro Calculator

  1. Enter your age and select your biological sex — both are required for the Mifflin-St Jeor BMR formula.

  2. Select your preferred height unit (cm or ft/in) and enter your height. Values convert automatically when you switch units.

  3. Select your preferred weight unit (kg or lbs) and enter your current body weight. Values convert automatically when you switch units.

  4. Select your activity level from the dropdown. Choose the option that most honestly reflects your average week — most people overestimate activity.

  5. Choose your goal: Lose Fat, Maintain Weight, or Build Muscle. Macro targets are adjusted to match your goal.

  6. Optionally, indicate whether you know your body fat percentage. If yes, enter it so macros can be calculated relative to your lean body mass.

  7. Click Calculate Macros to see your calorie target, protein range, fat range, carbohydrate range, meal split, expected progress and food source examples.
