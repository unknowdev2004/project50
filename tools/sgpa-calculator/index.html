    What Is SGPA?

    SGPA stands for Semester Grade Point Average. It is a single number that summarizes how you performed across all the subjects you took in one semester, weighted by how many credits each subject carries. A subject worth 4 credits influences your SGPA more than a subject worth 2 credits, because it represents a heavier share of your academic workload.

    SGPA is calculated fresh for every semester. Unlike CGPA, which accumulates across your entire degree, SGPA resets each term and reflects only that semester's performance — useful for tracking how you're trending from one term to the next.

    Many universities, especially those using 10-point grading systems common across India and similar systems elsewhere, report SGPA on semester mark sheets before combining every semester's SGPA into a final CGPA at graduation.

    How Is SGPA Calculated?

    The standard SGPA formula is straightforward:

    SGPA = Σ(Credits × Grade Point) ÷ Σ(Credits)

    Here is a practical example with four subjects on the 10-point scale:

    • Mathematics: 4 credits, grade A+ (9 points) → 36 weighted points
    • Physics: 3 credits, grade A (8 points) → 24 weighted points
    • Programming: 3 credits, grade O (10 points) → 30 weighted points
    • English: 2 credits, grade B+ (7 points) → 14 weighted points

    Total weighted points: 104. Total credits: 12. SGPA = 104 ÷ 12 = 8.67.

    Notice this differs from a simple average of the four grade points (9 + 8 + 10 + 7 ÷ 4 = 8.50). The weighted formula is correct because it accounts for each subject's credit load — heavier subjects should influence the result more than lighter ones.

    This calculator applies the formula using full floating-point precision internally, rounding only the final displayed SGPA to two decimal places.

    How To Use This Calculator

    1. Choose your grading scale — 10-Point or 4-Point — using the selector at the top. Pick whichever matches your institution's grade sheet.

    2. Enter the name of your first subject, such as "Mathematics" or "Data Structures".

    3. Enter the credit value for that subject, exactly as listed on your syllabus or transcript.

    4. Select the grade you received. The grade point is calculated automatically — it is not editable directly.

    5. Click Add Subject to add a row for your next subject and repeat for every subject in the semester.

    6. Click Calculate SGPA to see your result, academic summary, performance analysis, subject breakdown and insights.

    7. Click Reset to clear all entries and start over. Your data is also erased from browser storage.

    Your inputs are automatically saved to your browser as you type, so you can close the page and return later without re-entering your data.

    Understanding Your Results

    After calculating, this tool shows five result sections:

    Your SGPA — The primary result. Your weighted Semester Grade Point Average on the scale you selected.

    Academic Summary — Three key figures: total subjects entered, total credits across the semester, and total weighted grade points (the numerator in the SGPA formula). Use these to verify your inputs before accepting the result.

    Performance Analysis — Your unweighted average grade point (simple mean across subjects), your highest-scoring subject, and your lowest-scoring subject, so you can see which subjects lifted or lowered your SGPA.

    Subject Breakdown — A row-by-row table showing exactly how each subject's credits and grade point combined to produce its weighted points, so you can trace the full calculation.

    Insights — Contextual observations, such as whether your highest-credit subject is helping or hurting your SGPA, and whether your grades are consistent or vary significantly across subjects.

    Grade Scales and Grading Systems

    This calculator supports two commonly used grade scales as a convenience — they are general references, not universal standards:

    • 10-Point Scale — O (10), A+ (9), A (8), B+ (7), B (6), C (5), P (4), F (0). Widely used across many Indian universities and similar systems.
    • 4-Point Scale — A+ (4.0), A (3.7), B+ (3.3), B (3.0), B- (2.7), C+ (2.3), C (2.0), D (1.0), F (0). A common pattern in US-style grading, though exact point values vary by institution.

    Universities set their own official grading policies, and grade-to-point mappings can differ even between institutions that both call themselves "10-point" or "4-point" systems. Always check your institution's official grading table before treating this calculator's result as final.

    If your institution's scale doesn't match either option exactly, use the closest equivalent grade point, or treat the result as an approximation rather than an official figure.

    How Credits Affect Your SGPA

    Credits represent the weight or workload of a subject, and they directly determine how much influence that subject has on your SGPA. A 4-credit core subject moves your SGPA twice as much as a 2-credit elective, even if both subjects received the same grade.

    High-credit subjects matter most. If your heaviest subjects — typically core or major courses — earn strong grades, your SGPA benefits disproportionately. Conversely, a weak grade in a high-credit subject drags your SGPA down more than the same grade in a lighter subject.

    Total credits form the denominator. Adding more subjects doesn't automatically raise or lower your SGPA — what matters is the grade point each subject contributes relative to its share of your total credit load.

    Because of this, many students prioritize consistent performance in high-credit core subjects over squeezing out a marginally better grade in a low-credit elective.

    Difference Between SGPA and CGPA

    SGPA measures a single semester. It is calculated from that semester's subjects, weighted by each subject's credits, and it resets every term.

    CGPA is the cumulative weighted average of your SGPA across every semester you've completed, weighted by each semester's total credits. It is CGPA — not any single semester's SGPA — that typically appears on your final degree transcript.

    A strong SGPA in one semester doesn't guarantee a strong CGPA if earlier semesters were weaker, because CGPA accounts for your entire academic history. If you want to track your cumulative standing across multiple semesters, use the CGPA Calculator alongside this tool.

    Common Mistakes and Tips to Improve Your SGPA

    Mistake 1 — Mixing up scales. Entering grades on the wrong scale — for example selecting 4-point grades when your institution actually uses a 10-point system — produces a meaningless result. Always confirm your scale first.

    Mistake 2 — Leaving out a failed subject. Some students skip entering a subject they failed, hoping to see a higher SGPA. This is inaccurate — official transcripts always include failed subjects in the calculation, and this calculator does the same.

    Mistake 3 — Entering the wrong credit value. Double-check each subject's credit value against your syllabus. A single incorrect credit value can meaningfully shift your SGPA, especially for high-credit subjects.

    Tip: Focus effort on high-credit subjects. Because credits determine weighting, improving your grade in a high-credit core subject raises your SGPA more than the same improvement in a low-credit elective.

    Tip: Recalculate as grades are released. Update this calculator as individual subject grades come in during the semester to track your projected SGPA before final results are official.

    Tip: Watch your lowest-performing subject. The Performance Analysis section highlights your lowest grade point — if that subject also carries high credits, it's the single best place to focus improvement next semester.
