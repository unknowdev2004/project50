About the Body Fat Calculator

Body fat percentage is one of the most meaningful indicators of health and fitness. Unlike BMI, which only considers height and weight, body fat percentage tells you exactly how much of your body is fat tissue versus lean tissue — muscle, bone, organs and water. This distinction matters enormously: two people can share the same BMI yet have entirely different body compositions.

This calculator uses the U.S. Navy body fat formula, developed by Hodgdon and Beckett and widely used by military and fitness professionals. The method uses simple circumference measurements — neck, waist, and (for females) hip — to estimate body fat percentage. It avoids the need for expensive equipment like DEXA scans or hydrostatic weighing while providing a reliable estimate for the general population.

Beyond a single number, this tool calculates your fat mass (the weight of stored fat), lean body mass (everything else), your position within established healthy ranges, and — if you set a target — an estimated weight projection based on preserving lean mass.

How To Use This Body Fat Calculator

  1. Select your gender — the formula differs for males and females (females include a hip measurement).

  2. Enter your height. Use the unit toggle to switch between cm and ft/in. Stand straight without shoes.

  3. Measure your neck circumference just below the larynx (Adam's apple), sloping slightly downward. Enter in cm.

  4. Measure your waist at the narrowest point — typically just above the navel. Keep the tape parallel to the floor. Enter in cm.

  5. If you are female, measure your hip circumference at the widest point. Enter in cm.

  6. Optionally enter your current weight (kg or lbs) to unlock the fat mass and lean body mass breakdown.

  7. Optionally enter a target body fat % to see your estimated goal weight.

  8. Click Calculate Body Fat to see your full body composition report.

For the most accurate results, take measurements first thing in the morning before eating or drinking, and use a non-elastic tape measure held snugly but not compressing the skin.

Frequently Asked Questions

The Navy method has a margin of error of approximately 3–4% compared to gold-standard DEXA scanning. For most people it is a reliable and practical estimate — especially when measurements are taken correctly and consistently. It is less accurate for people with unusual fat distribution patterns (e.g. very high abdominal fat with low limb fat). Consistency matters more than absolute accuracy: tracking your measurements over time tells you whether you are trending in the right direction.
Healthy ranges differ by gender because women carry more essential fat for hormonal and reproductive function. For men, the fitness range is roughly 14–17% and the average healthy range is 18–24%. For women, the fitness range is approximately 21–24% and the healthy average range is 25–31%. Going below essential fat levels (below 5% for men, below 10–13% for women) can impair vital organ function. These are guidelines — your ideal range also depends on age, activity level and health history.
Women typically store more fat in the hip and thigh region (gynoid distribution), whereas men tend to store more fat in the abdominal region (android distribution). The Navy formula accounts for this biological difference by including hip circumference in the female calculation. Without it, the formula would underestimate female body fat in women with a lower waist-to-hip ratio.
The target weight projection uses lean body mass as a fixed anchor. The formula is: Goal Weight = Lean Body Mass ÷ (1 − Target Body Fat % ÷ 100). This assumes all weight loss comes from fat, with zero loss of lean mass — the ideal scenario. In practice, some lean mass loss occurs, so the real target weight may be slightly higher. The projection is best used as a directional goal, not an exact endpoint.
