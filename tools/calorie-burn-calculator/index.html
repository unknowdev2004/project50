About the Calorie Burn Calculator

A calorie burn calculator estimates how much energy — measured in kilocalories — your body uses during a specific activity. Every movement you make, from a gentle walk to an intense HIIT session, requires energy beyond what your body uses at rest. This tool helps you put a number on that extra energy expenditure.

Calorie expenditure during exercise depends on three main factors: the intensity of the activity, your body weight, and how long you do it for. Heavier bodies require more energy to move, more intense activities demand more energy per minute, and longer durations simply accumulate more total energy use.

Different activities burn calories at very different rates because they place different demands on your muscles and cardiovascular system. A slow walk keeps your body close to its resting energy use, while fast running, HIIT or CrossFit can push your energy expenditure many times higher than resting levels. This calculator uses standardised intensity values, known as MET values, to capture these differences across walking, running, cycling, gym workouts and sports.

What Are Calories Burned?

Calories are a unit of energy. Your body constantly uses energy to keep you alive — for breathing, circulation and cell repair — and uses additional energy whenever you move. The combination of these two is your total energy expenditure for the day.

When you exercise, your muscles demand extra energy on top of your resting needs. This additional usage is what we refer to as "calories burned" during exercise. The harder and longer you work, the more energy your body draws on to fuel that effort.

Different activities place different demands on the body. A slow walk uses muscles gently and for short bursts of effort, while running, cycling at speed, or high-intensity training recruit more muscle mass and push your heart rate higher, which increases the rate at which calories are burned.

How Calorie Burn Is Calculated

This calculator uses the MET-based formula, a widely used method in exercise science for estimating energy expenditure: Calories Burned = MET × Weight (kg) × Duration (hours).

MET (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) is a standardised measure of how intense an activity is compared to resting. A MET of 1 represents your resting energy use, while higher MET values represent progressively more demanding activities. Each activity in this calculator — from slow walking to HIIT — has its own established MET value.

Weight is included because moving a heavier body requires more energy. The formula is expressed in kilograms, so if you select pounds, your weight is converted automatically.

Duration is converted from minutes to hours before being applied, since MET values represent energy use per hour. Longer sessions accumulate proportionally more total calories burned.

Factors That Affect Calories Burned

Body Weight. Heavier bodies burn more calories doing the same activity for the same duration, because moving more mass requires more energy.

Activity Intensity. Higher-MET activities, such as fast running, HIIT or CrossFit, burn calories at a much faster rate per minute than lower-intensity activities like slow walking.

Duration. Total calories burned scale directly with how long you exercise. A longer session at a lower intensity can burn a similar total to a shorter session at a higher intensity.

Exercise Efficiency. Fitness level, technique and movement efficiency can all influence real-world calorie burn. More experienced athletes may move more efficiently, which can slightly reduce energy use for the same activity compared to beginners.

Understanding Your Results

Calories Burned is your total estimated energy expenditure for the session, based on MET, weight and duration.

Fat Equivalent converts your calorie burn into grams of body fat, using the common estimate of 7,700 kcal per kilogram of fat.

Calories Per Hour normalises your burn rate to an hourly figure, useful for comparing activities of different lengths.

Weekly and Monthly Projections assume you repeat this session three times per week, showing the cumulative effect over a week and a month.

Food Equivalents translate your calorie burn into everyday food items, helping you visualise the energy in practical terms.

Compare Activities shows how your same weight and duration would translate into calories burned across walking, running, cycling and HIIT, helping you see the relative impact of activity choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

The number of calories you burn depends on the activity's intensity (its MET value), your body weight and how long you exercise. This calculator multiplies the MET value of your chosen activity by your weight in kilograms and the duration in hours to estimate calories burned.
MET stands for Metabolic Equivalent of Task. One MET represents the energy you use at rest. An activity with a MET of 8 burns roughly eight times as much energy as resting. MET values let researchers and calculators compare the intensity of different activities on a common scale.
Moving a heavier body requires more energy. Since the calorie burn formula multiplies MET by body weight, a heavier person burns more calories than a lighter person doing the same activity for the same duration, even though the relative effort may feel similar.
MET-based calculators provide useful estimates based on population averages, but actual calorie burn varies with fitness level, technique, terrain, temperature and individual metabolism. Treat the results as a strong starting point rather than an exact figure, and use them mainly to compare activities relative to each other.
Generally, higher-intensity activities with higher MET values burn more calories per minute. Fast running, HIIT and CrossFit tend to burn the most per minute, while slow walking burns the least. However, total calories also depend heavily on duration, so a longer lower-intensity session can match a shorter high-intensity one.
Exercise increases the calories you burn, which can contribute to a calorie deficit, but weight loss ultimately depends on total calories consumed versus total calories burned. Combining regular exercise with mindful eating tends to be far more effective than relying on exercise alone.
One kilogram of body fat is commonly estimated to be equivalent to about 7,700 kilocalories. This figure is used in this calculator to estimate the potential monthly fat loss from a given calorie burn, assuming calorie intake stays unchanged. Actual results vary by individual.
Food equivalents translate an abstract calorie number into something tangible, like a number of bananas, eggs, samosas or cups of rice. This can help put your calorie burn into everyday perspective. They are for illustration only and are not intended as a meal plan or dietary recommendation.
