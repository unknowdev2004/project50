    About the Daily Calorie Planner

    A calorie is a unit of energy. Your body burns calories constantly — to keep your heart beating, your lungs breathing, and your cells functioning. Understanding how many calories you need each day is the foundation of any successful nutrition plan, whether your goal is to lose weight, maintain your current weight, or build muscle.

    BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest over 24 hours — the energy cost of simply being alive. This planner calculates your BMR using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which is considered the most accurate formula for the general population by sports medicine researchers.

    TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor. It represents your total calorie burn across an average day, including exercise, walking, and general movement. Eating at your TDEE maintains your current weight.

    To lose fat, you eat less than your TDEE (a calorie deficit). To build muscle, you eat more than your TDEE (a calorie surplus). This planner applies evidence-based deficit and surplus values: a moderate 500 kcal/day deficit for fat loss (approximately 0.45 kg/week) and a moderate 300 kcal/day surplus for muscle gain — ranges that support results without being extreme.

    How To Use This Daily Calorie Planner

    1. Enter your age and select your biological sex — these are used in the Mifflin-St Jeor BMR formula.

    2. Select your preferred height unit (cm or ft/in), then enter your height. Select your preferred weight unit (kg or lbs) and enter your current weight.

    3. Select your activity level from the dropdown. Choose the option that best reflects your average week — be honest, most people overestimate activity.

    4. Choose your goal: Lose Fat, Maintain Weight, or Build Muscle. The tool will adjust your calorie recommendation accordingly.

    5. Optionally, enter a goal weight to see an estimated timeline for reaching it based on your calorie plan.

    6. Click Calculate My Calories to see your BMR, TDEE, recommended daily calories, macro breakdown, and monthly projection.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is validated by peer-reviewed research and is consistently rated as the most accurate BMR formula for most adults. That said, all calorie formulas are estimates — individual metabolism can vary by ±10% due to genetics, hormones, muscle mass, and other factors. Use this as a strong starting point, then adjust based on real-world results over 2–4 weeks.
    TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure. It is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor that accounts for how much you move throughout the day. A sedentary person multiplies their BMR by 1.2; an athlete doing intense daily training multiplies by 1.9. Eating at your exact TDEE keeps your weight stable. Eating below it creates a deficit; above it creates a surplus.
    A deficit of 300–500 kcal per day is widely considered safe and sustainable. This typically produces 0.3–0.5 kg of weight loss per week. Deficits larger than 700 kcal/day increase the risk of muscle loss, nutrient deficiencies, fatigue, and rebound weight gain. This planner uses a moderate 500 kcal deficit, which sits in the proven sweet spot for most people.
