    What Is Attendance Percentage?

    Attendance percentage is the proportion of held classes a student has actually attended, expressed out of 100. It is the single number most institutions use to decide whether a student is eligible to sit for exams, receive internal marks, or continue enrolment in good standing.

    Unlike a raw class count, attendance percentage automatically adjusts as more classes are held, which means it can move even on days you do not attend anything — a class held while you are away lowers your percentage even though you took no new action. This is why tracking attendance proactively, rather than only checking it before exams, helps students stay ahead of eligibility problems.

    Most universities, colleges and schools worldwide use attendance percentage as a hard eligibility gate, commonly set between 65% and 85% depending on the institution and course.

    Attendance Formula

    The core formula is straightforward:

    Attendance % = (Classes Attended ÷ Total Classes) × 100

    For example, if you have attended 34 out of 40 classes held so far: 34 ÷ 40 = 0.85, so your attendance is 85%.

    This calculator also determines two forward-looking figures using the same underlying logic:

    • Classes you can still miss — the largest number of additional classes you can skip, assuming your total keeps growing, while staying at or above your required percentage.
    • Consecutive classes needed — if you are below the requirement, how many classes in a row you must attend without missing any to bring your percentage back up to the required threshold.

    All calculations are performed using full floating-point precision internally. Only the final displayed values are rounded.

    How To Use This Calculator

    1. Enter the number of classes you have attended so far, as a whole number.

    2. Enter the total number of classes held so far for that subject or course, as a whole number.

    3. Enter your institution's required attendance percentage — this defaults to 75%, a common threshold, but you can change it to match your own institution's policy.

    4. Click Calculate Attendance to see your current percentage, eligibility, forecast and how many classes you can miss or must attend.

    5. Click Reset to clear all fields and start over. Your saved data is also cleared from browser storage.

    Your inputs are automatically saved to your browser as you type, so you can close the page and return later without re-entering your data.

    Understanding Your Results

    After calculating, this tool shows six result sections:

    Your Current Attendance — the primary result. Your exact attendance percentage right now, along with an eligibility status.

    Eligibility — a clear eligible / not eligible badge, with a visual bar showing where your current attendance sits relative to your required threshold.

    Attendance Analysis — the raw counts behind every calculation: classes attended, total classes, and classes missed.

    Insights — contextual observations that translate the numbers above into plain-language guidance about your current standing and what to do next.

    Attendance Rules and Common Requirements

    Attendance policies vary widely, but a few patterns are common across most institutions:

    • 75% is the most common threshold — widely used across universities in India and many other countries as the minimum to sit for final exams.
    • Some institutions allow condonation — a small buffer (for example 65–75%) may still be accepted with a documented medical or personal reason, subject to approval.
    • Attendance is usually tracked per subject, not as one overall number — you may be eligible in one course and short in another, so calculate each subject separately.
    • Late arrivals may count as partial or zero attendance depending on the institution's specific rules — check your handbook for how lateness is treated.
    • Attendance requirements typically cannot be waived retroactively — the only way to raise a low percentage is to attend future classes consistently, since missed classes cannot be un-missed.

    Tips For Staying Eligible

    Recalculate after every missed class. A single missed class can shift your "classes you can still miss" figure more than expected, especially early in a course when the total class count is still small.

    Build a buffer early. Attendance is easiest to protect when your total class count is low. Missing classes early in a course has a smaller mathematical impact than missing the same number later, so avoid using up your buffer too soon.

    Check the forecast before skipping. Use the Attendance Forecast section before deciding to miss a class — seeing the exact percentage drop in advance often changes the decision.

    Track each subject separately. If you take multiple courses with independent attendance requirements, run this calculator once per subject rather than combining totals.

    Common Mistakes

    Mistake 1 — Confusing total classes with classes scheduled for the term. Enter only the classes actually held so far, not the total planned for the whole semester. The percentage should reflect what has happened, not what is planned.

    Mistake 2 — Assuming missed classes can be "made up" mathematically. Once a class is missed, it permanently lowers the denominator's contribution. The only way forward is attending future classes — there is no way to retroactively remove a miss from the calculation.

    Mistake 3 — Waiting until the requirement is missed to check. Checking your "classes you can still miss" figure regularly, rather than only before exams, gives you time to correct course while it is still mathematically possible.

    Mistake 4 — Ignoring per-subject requirements. A student can be comfortably eligible overall while being short in one specific subject. Always check subject-level attendance, not just an overall average.
