      What Is CGPA to Percentage Conversion?

      CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) and percentage are two different ways of expressing academic performance. Many boards and universities report results as a CGPA, but job applications, higher-education admissions, and some competitive exams ask for a percentage — so students frequently need to convert between the two.

      The catch is that there is no single formula that works everywhere. Each board or university defines its own conversion rule, and some don't publish one at all. This tool gives you three ways to get an accurate result: verified formulas from specific institutions, general reference formulas for a quick estimate, and a custom formula builder for anything not listed.

      How Does CGPA to Percentage Conversion Work?

      Every formula supported here follows the same safe, linear shape:

      Percentage = (CGPA × multiplier) + offset

      The multiplier scales your CGPA up to a percentage-like range, and an optional offset shifts the result. For example, CGPA × 9.5 uses a multiplier of 9.5 and no offset, so a CGPA of 8.2 converts to 77.90%. A formula like (CGPA − 0.75) × 10 is mathematically the same shape with a multiplier of 10 and an offset of −7.5. Restricting every formula to this structure keeps the calculation transparent and reversible, and avoids the risks of evaluating arbitrary, unverified expressions.

      Why Different Institutions Use Different Formulas

      Grading systems are set independently by each board, university, or programme — there is no central authority standardising how a 10-point or 4-point CGPA maps to a percentage. A conversion formula that one university publishes officially may have no relationship at all to another university's rule, even if both use a 10-point scale.

      This is why the same CGPA can produce noticeably different percentages depending on which formula is applied. A 10-point CGPA of 8.2, for instance, converts to 77.90% under CGPA × 9.5 but 82.00% under CGPA × 10. Neither number is incorrect — they simply reflect different institutional policies.

      How to Choose the Correct Conversion Formula

      1. Check your marksheet, transcript, or official grading policy document first — many institutions print the exact conversion formula there.

      2. If your institution appears under Verified Institution in the selector above, use that preset and read its applicability note before relying on the result.

      3. If it isn't listed, use General Formula only for a rough estimate — it is explicitly not tied to any specific institution.

      4. For an exact result from an unlisted institution, use Custom Formula and enter the multiplier and offset from your own official documentation.

      5. For applications, admissions, or transcripts, always get a formula confirmed by your institution's examination office rather than relying solely on any online calculator, including this one.

      How to Convert Percentage Back to CGPA

      Because every supported formula has the shape Percentage = (CGPA × multiplier) + offset, it can always be algebraically reversed to CGPA = (Percentage − offset) ÷ multiplier, as long as the multiplier isn't zero. Switch to the Percentage → CGPA tab, keep the same conversion method selected, enter your percentage, and the tool applies the reverse formula automatically.

      If a custom formula's multiplier is exactly zero, that division is mathematically undefined — the percentage would be the same constant no matter what CGPA was entered — so the tool disables reverse conversion for that case and shows a clear message instead of a misleading number.

      Understanding Institutional Conversion Rules

      The Verified Institution presets in this tool are included only where a source and applicability could be reasonably identified — each one shows its formula, source, and applicability notes directly beneath the selector. These are still general references rather than guaranteed matches for every regulation year or programme at that institution, which is why each preset carries its own caveat.

      The General Formula options (CGPA × 9.5, CGPA × 10) are not tied to any institution at all. They exist for a fast approximate answer when you don't yet know your institution's exact rule, and are clearly labelled as general, indicative calculations rather than official ones.

      Common CGPA Conversion Mistakes

      Mistake 1 — Assuming one formula applies everywhere. CGPA × 9.5 and CGPA × 10 are both widely repeated online, but neither is universal. Using the wrong one can misrepresent your percentage by several points.

      Mistake 2 — Mixing up the CGPA scale. Entering a 4-point CGPA while a 10-point formula is selected (or vice versa) produces a meaningless result. Always confirm the scale first.

      Mistake 3 — Treating a general or custom result as official. Only your institution's own examination office can certify a percentage for official use — any calculator result, verified or not, should be treated as a reference.

      Tips for Using CGPA Conversions Correctly

      Use the Compare Other Conversion Methods section to see how much your result would change under a different formula — this is especially useful when you're unsure which one your institution actually uses. If you're applying somewhere that asks for a specific formula (a scholarship, an employer, a foreign university), use Custom Formula with their exact multiplier and offset rather than guessing from a general preset.

      Keep a copy of your official conversion certificate or marksheet alongside any calculator result — for high-stakes uses like visa applications or accreditation, institutions typically want the certified document, not a self-calculated figure.
