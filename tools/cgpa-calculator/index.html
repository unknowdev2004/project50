    What Is CGPA?

    CGPA stands for Cumulative Grade Point Average. It is the single number that represents your overall academic performance across every semester you have completed. Unlike a semester GPA — which reflects only one term — CGPA gives universities, employers and graduate schools a comprehensive view of your academic history from day one to your most recent results.

    CGPA is calculated using a weighted average, where each semester's GPA is multiplied by its credit hours. This ensures that a semester carrying 24 credits counts more than a lighter semester with 12 credits, which accurately reflects the true academic load you carried at each stage of your degree.

    Most universities around the world — including institutions in India, Pakistan, the US, UK, Canada and Australia — use CGPA as the primary academic metric on official transcripts. It is the number most employers ask for on job applications, and the figure used to determine scholarship eligibility, academic honours, and progression to postgraduate study.

    How Is CGPA Calculated?

    The standard CGPA formula is straightforward:

    CGPA = Σ(GPA × Credits) ÷ Σ(Credits)

    Here is a practical example. Suppose you have completed four semesters:

    • Semester 1: GPA 3.8, 18 credits → 68.4 weighted points
    • Semester 2: GPA 3.5, 20 credits → 70.0 weighted points
    • Semester 3: GPA 3.9, 18 credits → 70.2 weighted points
    • Semester 4: GPA 3.6, 20 credits → 72.0 weighted points

    Total weighted points: 280.6. Total credits: 76. CGPA = 280.6 ÷ 76 = 3.69.

    Notice that simply averaging the four GPAs (3.8 + 3.5 + 3.9 + 3.6 ÷ 4 = 3.70) gives a slightly different result. The weighted formula is always correct because it accounts for the different credit loads each semester carries.

    This calculator applies the formula precisely using full floating-point precision internally, rounding only the final displayed value to two decimal places.

    How To Use This Calculator

    1. Enter the GPA for your first semester. Use the same scale your university uses — 4.0, 5.0, 7.0 or 10.0 all work correctly.

    2. Enter the total credit hours for that semester. If you are unsure, check your transcript or course catalogue — typical semesters carry 15–24 credits.

    3. Optionally, give the semester a name (e.g. "Year 1 Sem 1") to make results easier to read. This field is not required.

    4. Click Add Semester to add a row for your next semester and repeat the process for each completed term.

    5. Click Calculate CGPA to see your cumulative result, academic summary, performance analysis and insights.

    6. Click Reset to clear all entries and start over. Your data is also erased from browser storage.

    Your inputs are automatically saved to your browser as you type, so you can close the page and return later without re-entering your data.

    Understanding Your Results

    After calculating, this tool shows four result sections:

    Your CGPA — The primary result. Your weighted cumulative grade point average, followed by an academic classification such as Excellent, Very Good or Satisfactory based on your score.

    Academic Summary — Three key figures: total semesters entered, total credit hours across all semesters, and total weighted grade points (the numerator in the CGPA formula). These help verify that your inputs are correct before accepting the result.

    Performance Analysis — Your unweighted average GPA (simple arithmetic mean across semesters), your highest single-semester GPA, and your lowest. The semester name is shown alongside each to help you identify which term performed best and which dragged your average down.

    Insights — Contextual observations about your academic trajectory. For example, whether your performance is consistent or variable, whether your most recent semester improved or fell below your CGPA, and what a strong finish might mean for your final GPA.

    GPA Scales and Grading Systems

    This calculator is scale-neutral — it works correctly for any GPA system. The most common scales include:

    • 4.0 Scale — Used widely in the US, Canada, and many Southeast Asian universities. An A typically equals 4.0.
    • 5.0 Scale — Used in some Australian and West African institutions. An A may equal 5.0 or 7.0 depending on the system.
    • 7.0 Scale — Common in Australian universities like the University of Melbourne. High Distinction is typically a 7.
    • 10.0 Scale — Standard in India, used across most Indian universities. An O (Outstanding) grade is 10.

    Simply enter your GPA using whatever scale your institution uses. Do not convert to a different scale — the CGPA formula works identically regardless of the maximum value, and mixed conversions introduce errors.

    If your institution uses letter grades (A, B, C), convert them to numeric GPA using your institution's official conversion table first. Different universities assign different numeric values to the same letter grade.

    Factors That Affect Your CGPA

    Several variables determine how quickly your CGPA changes from semester to semester:

    Credit Weight. High-credit semesters have the biggest impact on your CGPA. A 3.0 GPA in a semester with 24 credits shifts your cumulative average more than a 3.0 GPA in a semester with 12 credits. This is why late semesters, which often carry the heaviest loads, can significantly influence your final CGPA.

    Starting Performance. Your earliest semesters become a baseline that is difficult to escape. A poor first year lowers your CGPA in a way that takes several strong subsequent semesters to correct — because each early semester permanently forms part of the weighted pool.

    Course Difficulty. Elective courses in your strongest subjects can lift your GPA above what core required courses achieve. Some students strategically balance heavier required modules with lighter electives to manage their overall GPA.

    Grade Replacement Policies. Some universities allow students to retake failed or low-grade subjects and replace the original grade in the CGPA calculation. If your institution offers this, it can be one of the most effective ways to improve a historically low CGPA.

    Pass/Fail and Audit Courses. Courses taken as Pass/Fail or audited are typically excluded from the GPA calculation. If you have such courses, do not include them in this calculator as they would distort your CGPA.

    Common Mistakes and Tips

    Mistake 1 — Mixing GPA scales. If your first two semesters were graded on a 4.0 scale but your next semesters use a 10.0 scale, never enter them together as-is. The calculator would weight them incorrectly. Convert all to one consistent scale first.

    Mistake 2 — Using subject credits instead of semester credits. Enter the total credit hours for the entire semester, not for any single subject. Mixing the two will inflate your total credits and produce an inaccurate result.

    Mistake 3 — Including incomplete or pending grades. Only include semesters where final grades have been officially awarded. Estimated or projected grades will reduce accuracy.

    Tip: Focus on high-credit semesters. Because credit hours drive the weighting, earning a strong GPA in your highest-credit semesters has a disproportionate positive effect on your CGPA. Prioritize effort in your most credit-heavy terms.

    Tip: Track your trajectory. Use this calculator each semester after results are released. Watching your CGPA move over time reveals patterns — consistent improvement, stagnation, or a specific semester that needs attention.

    Tip: Simulate future semesters. Add a hypothetical semester with an estimated GPA to see how it would move your CGPA. This helps you set realistic targets for the terms ahead.
