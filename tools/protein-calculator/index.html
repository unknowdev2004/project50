About This Tool

The Protein Calculator estimates your ideal daily protein intake based on your bodyweight, dietary goal and activity level. It uses evidence-based multipliers from sports nutrition research to give you three targets: a minimum floor to preserve muscle, a recommended sweet spot supported by the scientific consensus, and an optimal ceiling for those seeking maximum results.

Unlike calculators that output a single number, this tool shows how your protein needs shift across goals and activity levels — so you understand the reasoning, not just the result.

How To Use

  1. Enter your bodyweight in kg or lbs. Switch units using the toggle beside the label.
  2. Choose your primary goal: Maintenance, Fat Loss, or Muscle Gain.
  3. Select your activity level — honest assessment gives more useful results.
  4. Tap Calculate Protein. Your minimum, recommended and optimal targets appear instantly.
  5. Use the Protein Per Meal card to distribute intake across 3–6 meals.
  6. Use the Food Equivalents card to plan meals around real food sources.

Why Protein Matters

Protein is the primary building block of muscle, enzymes, hormones and immune cells. It is also the most satiating macronutrient — higher protein diets consistently reduce hunger and improve dietary adherence. Unlike carbohydrates and fat, protein cannot be stored by the body, making daily intake targets meaningful rather than optional guidelines.

Protein also has the highest thermic effect of food (TEF) — approximately 20–30% of calories from protein are used in digestion and absorption, compared to 5–10% for carbohydrates and 0–3% for fat. This makes protein the single most impactful macronutrient for body composition.

Protein For Fat Loss

During a calorie deficit, the body turns to muscle protein for energy. Research shows that consuming 1.6–2.2g of protein per kg of bodyweight during a cut significantly reduces muscle breakdown compared to lower intakes. Higher protein also keeps hunger lower, making adherence to a calorie deficit far more sustainable.

A 2020 meta-analysis in the British Journal of Sports Medicine found that dietary protein supplementation during caloric restriction produced significantly greater fat-free mass retention than lower-protein approaches. Fat loss targets in this calculator start at 1.6g/kg minimum for this reason.

Protein For Muscle Gain

For muscle hypertrophy, the research consensus is 1.6–2.4g/kg/day. The lower end (1.6g/kg) is adequate for most trained individuals in a modest calorie surplus. The higher end (2.2–2.4g/kg) is appropriate for experienced athletes, those on aggressive training programmes, or individuals seeking to maximise anabolic stimulus.

Protein timing also matters for muscle gain: spreading intake across 4–6 meals of approximately 0.4g/kg each appears to saturate muscle protein synthesis more effectively than eating the same total protein in fewer, larger meals.

Understanding Your Results

The three targets represent different levels of evidence-based commitment. The Minimum is the floor below which muscle preservation or growth becomes significantly compromised. The Recommended target reflects the scientific consensus for your goal and activity level — aim here by default. The Optimal target provides additional headroom for those with very high training volumes or who want to push beyond the consensus.

Activity bonuses add to the base goal factor: athletes receive up to +0.4g/kg above the goal baseline, reflecting the greater protein turnover from high training volumes. All values are capped at 350g/day, a practical limit beyond which additional protein provides minimal additional benefit for most people.

Common Protein Sources

Chicken breast provides around 31g of protein per 100g — one of the highest protein-to-calorie ratios of any whole food. Eggs deliver 6g each with a complete amino acid profile. Greek yoghurt provides 10g/100g and is convenient for breakfast or snacks. Milk offers 3.4g per 100ml. Lentils and chickpeas deliver 7–9g per 100g cooked and are excellent plant-based options. Whey protein supplements provide ~24g per standard scoop and are useful for hitting targets when whole food intake is insufficient.
