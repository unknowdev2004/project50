About This Tool

The Target Weight Timeline Calculator answers the question that every person with a weight goal eventually asks: how long is this actually going to take? Rather than giving you a single optimistic number, it generates a realistic timeline based on your personal physiology — your BMR, TDEE and activity level — not population averages.

The tool automatically detects your direction. If your target is below your current weight, it generates fat loss timelines. If your target is higher, it generates weight gain timelines. Both scenarios offer two strategies: a recommended sustainable pace and an accelerated option, so you can choose the approach that fits your lifestyle.

Results include a month-by-month milestone table so you always know where you should be — not just when you'll arrive. All calculations use the Mifflin-St Jeor BMR equation, 7,700 kcal per kg of body tissue, and enforce safety calorie floors of 1,500 kcal for men and 1,200 kcal for women. No data is ever sent to a server.

How To Use

  1. Select your gender — this affects the BMR constant in the Mifflin-St Jeor equation.

  2. Enter your age in years (15–100).

  3. Select your preferred height unit and enter your height. Values convert automatically when you switch units.

  4. Enter your current weight and then your target weight — in your preferred unit. The calculator automatically detects whether you're aiming to lose or gain weight.

  5. Choose your activity level. Be honest — most people overestimate. Sedentary is a desk job with little movement. Lightly Active is 1–3 casual workouts per week.

  6. Click Calculate Timeline to see your personalised timeline, monthly milestones, calorie targets and strategy comparison.

How the Timeline Is Calculated

The calculator uses a three-step process:

  1. BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor): Male = (10 × kg) + (6.25 × cm) − (5 × age) + 5. Female = (10 × kg) + (6.25 × cm) − (5 × age) − 161.

  2. TDEE: BMR × activity multiplier (Sedentary 1.2, Lightly Active 1.375, Moderately Active 1.55, Very Active 1.725, Athlete 1.9).

  3. Timeline: The chosen daily calorie adjustment (e.g. −500 kcal) is converted to a monthly weight-change rate using 7,700 kcal = 1 kg. The weight difference is divided by that monthly rate: Timeline (months) = weight difference ÷ monthly change rate.

For the example of 86 kg → 75 kg on a Lose Fat plan (−500 kcal/day, 1.95 kg/month): 11 kg ÷ 1.95 = 5.6 months, displayed as 6 months.

Fat Loss vs Weight Gain Strategies

The tool offers two strategies for each direction:

  • Lose Fat (−500 kcal/day): The standard recommendation — approximately 1.95 kg/month. Sustainable, minimises muscle loss.
  • Aggressive Fat Loss (−750 kcal/day): Approximately 2.92 kg/month. Faster results but harder to sustain and may increase muscle loss risk. Not recommended as a long-term strategy.
  • Lean Gain (+300 kcal/day): Approximately 1.17 kg/month — a slow, clean bulk with minimal fat accumulation. Recommended for most people building muscle.
  • Weight Gain (+500 kcal/day): Approximately 1.95 kg/month — faster mass gain but with proportionally more fat gain alongside muscle.

Setting Realistic Expectations

The timeline shown is based on consistent daily adherence to your calorie target. In practice, several factors cause real-world results to differ from the calculation:

  • Water retention: Scale weight fluctuates daily by 1–3 kg due to water, glycogen and sodium — unrelated to fat or muscle change.
  • Metabolic adaptation: Prolonged calorie restriction slightly lowers your TDEE over time. Recalculate every 4–6 weeks using your updated current weight.
  • Diet adherence: A single 1,000 kcal over-eating day undoes two days of a 500 kcal deficit. Consistency matters more than perfection.
  • Training: Resistance training during a fat loss phase helps preserve muscle mass and keeps your BMR higher — giving you a better body composition even at the same scale weight.

Use the milestones card to track your progress. If you're consistently below a milestone after 4 weeks, your actual TDEE may be lower than estimated — reduce by a further 100–200 kcal and recalculate.

Calorie Safety Floors

This calculator enforces minimum daily calorie intakes: 1,500 kcal for men and 1,200 kcal for women. These floors exist because eating below these levels risks nutrient deficiencies, significant muscle loss, immune suppression and metabolic slowdown.

In some edge cases — very low body weight, advanced age, very short stature or a sedentary lifestyle — your maintenance calories (TDEE) may already be at or below the floor. When this happens, the tool shows the "unavailable at safe calorie levels" message rather than displaying a misleading timeline. This is intentional. If you see this message, consult a healthcare provider or dietitian before attempting a calorie deficit.

Understanding Your Results

The Timeline Summary card shows your weight difference and the time to goal at each strategy. The recommended strategy is highlighted. Use this as your headline number.

The Monthly Milestones table shows where your weight should be at each month. For long journeys (over 8 months), only the first 6 milestones and the final goal are shown. Use this to check whether you're on track.

The Weekly Progress card gives the weekly rate and total weeks — more actionable for weekly weigh-ins than months.

The Calorie Targets card shows your maintenance calories and each strategy's daily target. These are the numbers to enter into your food tracking app.

The Strategy Comparison table lets you see side-by-side how the two strategies differ in timeline, monthly rate and weekly rate — useful for deciding which pace to commit to.
