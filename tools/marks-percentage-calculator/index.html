                What Is a Percentage Calculator?

                A marks percentage calculator converts raw exam or assignment scores into a percentage — the single number teachers, universities and employers use to compare performance across different tests, subjects and grading scales. Instead of manually working through the arithmetic every time a result comes in, this tool handles the calculation instantly and accurately.

                This calculator goes further than a basic percentage tool. It adapts to five common academic situations: a single quick result, a full subject list with different maximum marks, a target you are trying to reach, a best-of-N grading scheme, and a forward-looking goal plan — all inside one clean, connected calculator.

                How Is Percentage Calculated?

                The standard formula is straightforward:

                Percentage = (Marks Obtained ÷ Maximum Marks) × 100

                For example, a student who scores 425 marks out of a possible 500 has a percentage of (425 ÷ 500) × 100 = 85%. This calculator applies the formula using full floating-point precision internally, rounding only the final displayed value.

                How to Calculate Marks Percentage

                1. Choose the mode that matches what you need — Quick Calculate for a single result, or one of the other four modes for more detail.

                2. Enter your marks obtained and the maximum marks available. Decimal values are accepted for partial-mark grading.

                3. Click Calculate to see your percentage, a performance classification, and any relevant summary figures.

                4. Switch modes at any time using the tabs at the top — your entries in each mode are kept separately.

                5. Click Reset to clear the current mode only. Your data in the other modes is left untouched.

                Your inputs are automatically saved to your browser as you type, separately for each mode, so you can close the page and return later without re-entering your data.

                How to Calculate Percentage for Multiple Subjects

                When you have several subjects, each with its own obtained and maximum marks, the correct overall percentage is calculated by summing all obtained marks and all maximum marks first, then dividing:

                Overall % = (Σ Obtained Marks ÷ Σ Maximum Marks) × 100

                This is deliberately different from averaging each subject's individual percentage. Averaging treats every subject as equally weighted regardless of how many marks it was out of, which can distort the result when subjects have different maximum marks. Summing totals first always produces the mathematically accurate overall percentage, which is why the Multiple Subjects mode uses this method.

                How to Calculate Marks Needed for a Target

                The Reach a Target mode works backward from a goal percentage to tell you exactly what you still need to score:

                Required Total Marks = (Target % ÷ 100) × (Current Maximum + Remaining Maximum)

                Marks Needed = Required Total Marks − Current Obtained Marks

                Dividing the marks needed by the remaining maximum marks gives the percentage you must score in everything that is left. If that required percentage exceeds 100%, the target cannot be reached — this calculator flags that clearly instead of showing a misleading figure.

                Best-of-Subjects Percentage Explained

                Some grading systems — particularly in schools that count only your top-performing electives or optional subjects — calculate a final percentage using only the best N subjects out of a larger list. This calculator ranks every subject by its own percentage, from highest to lowest, and includes the top N in the final calculation.

                Ranking uses individual subject percentage, not raw marks, since a subject worth 50 marks is not directly comparable to one worth 100 marks by raw score alone. When two subjects tie exactly on percentage, the one with the higher maximum marks is ranked first; if that is also equal, the subject entered earliest is ranked first.

                How to Plan Your Exam Percentage

                Plan My Goal uses the same underlying mathematics as Reach a Target, but is framed around forward planning rather than an in-progress target. Enter your current obtained and maximum marks, the maximum marks still to come, and the final percentage you want to end up with. The calculator tells you whether that goal is already secured, still achievable, requires a perfect score, or is mathematically impossible from your current position — with plain-language guidance on what to do next.

                Revisit this mode after every new result to see how your required future percentage shifts. A strong early result lowers the bar for what is still needed; a weaker one raises it.

                Common Percentage Calculation Mistakes

                Mistake 1 — Averaging subject percentages instead of totals. When subjects have different maximum marks, averaging their individual percentages gives an incorrect overall figure. Always sum obtained and maximum marks first.

                Mistake 2 — Mixing subject credits with subject marks. In multi-subject mode, enter the maximum marks for the subject's assessment, not a credit-hour weighting from a separate grading system.

                Mistake 3 — Assuming a target is always achievable. If the required remaining percentage exceeds 100%, no score can close the gap. Check the status shown in Reach a Target or Plan My Goal before assuming a target is realistic.

                Mistake 4 — Ranking best subjects by raw marks. A subject scored out of 50 cannot be fairly compared to one scored out of 100 using raw marks alone — always rank by percentage.

                Tip: Recheck your maximum marks. A common source of error is entering the wrong maximum marks for an assessment — double-check the paper or syllabus before calculating.
